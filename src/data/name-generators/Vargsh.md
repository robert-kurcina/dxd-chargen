### Vargsh Name Generator
*For Vargsh/Jhastrujini names in the later imperial and courtly language. The oldest uVargsh speech remains beyond accurate Humaniki reproduction.*

The Vargsh call both their people and their native speech **Vargsh** in the surviving later records; context distinguishes the people from the language. **Ershthiikal** is the Humaniki/Lorespeak scholarly exonym for that language and its old imperial registers. It is unrelated to Ithuuikal despite the similar Humaniki `-iikal` gloss.

Ancient uVargsh speech used several coordinated channels at once: audible hissing and oral consonants, clicks and pops, plus low resonances below ordinary Humaniki speech. Red Offal genetic alteration before Osshiliikkal gradually made the Vargsh mouth, tongue, soft tissue, and hands more Humaniki-like. The speech changed with the body. Osshiliikkal and Mansiikal still depended heavily upon resonance; Irishtu increasingly converted those contrasts into audible consonant length and fricatives; Loash court speech could be spoken without the sub-sonic channel, although a Humaniki speaker remained obviously Accented.

In Common transcription, `ss` marks a long hiss, `sh` a broad hiss, `gh` a voiced deep fricative, and `kh` a voiceless deep fricative. Long or reinforced vowels in old records often represent a resonance distinction which a Humaniki scribe could hear but not reproduce. Do not treat these spellings as a complete native phonetic notation.

In uVargsh speech, resonance may distinguish two roots whose audible portion sounds identical to a Humaniki listener. Osshiliikkal and Mansiikal preserve this most strongly. Irishtu and Loash increasingly convert the distinction into audible length or deep-fricative contrasts.

##### UVARGSH FORMULATION

uVargsh is strongly agglutinative. Roots, relational forms, status markers, and qualifications accumulate into long formulations. The audible string may sound cumbersome to Humaniki because the final element can narrow, qualify, or force a reconsideration of an earlier element. A formal listener therefore waits for the formulation to close before answering.

The symbols below are **Common transcription conventions** for registers which were carried physically in ancestral speech. They are not native letters.

| Transcription | uVargsh register | Meaning in play |
|---|---|---|
| `!phrase!` | low-frequency emphasis | The enclosed formulation is pressed with emphasis. Most Humaniki do not hear the register. |
| `!!phrase!!` | reinforced emphasis | Stronger emotional or procedural force. |
| `!!!phrase!!!` | extreme emphasis | Exceptional force. Native written protocol normally leaves this degree to context rather than drawing additional emphasis lines. |
| `#phrase#` | low thrum plus aspirated hiss | The enclosed formulation is classified as important to the present claim, substantiation, salience, or concern. This is independent of emphasis. |
| `#!phrase!#` | combined salience and emphasis | The formulation is both controlling to the matter and forcefully pressed. |

A character without Jhastrujin hearing usually receives only the audible channel. **Detect > Hearing** may reveal that a low-frequency register is present, subject to the ordinary rules for that Trait, but detecting the vibration does not itself teach uVargsh or supply the missing cultural context.

##### COURTLY FLOATS

A formal courtly session before the **Reed Speaker** is known as a float. The Reed Speaker is the monarchical voice of the court and is usually female, though the position can be held by a male monarch, male consort, or female consort.

A courtier delivers a complete formulation while the court hears audible speech and feels the lower register. Hiss aspirations divide portions of the utterance. When the speaker is done, decorum requires silence while the other courtiers reconsider the whole formulation. Responses normally begin with those of highest rank or nearest standing to the Reed Speaker. An accidental early response is embarrassing but may be forgiven. A response which ignores protocol can draw a tail-lash and the hiss of higher-ranked courtiers, often carrying a register equivalent to `#!important consideration!#`, after which the court pauses and begins again in rank.

Courtiers often reproduce near-complete formulations from earlier floats in the manner of legal precedent. Because uVargsh meaning includes sound, vibration, rank, order, and remembered context, such a citation can reactivate a shared physical memory of an earlier proceeding. When the float is believed finished, the assembly waits through a deliberately long final pause so that no late speaker is excluded. A shared low thrum then precedes the near-simultaneous hiss by which the court agrees to adjourn.

##### CONCEPT-GLYPHS

uVargsh has no alphabet. Its oldest surviving records are semasiographic concept-glyphs made from simple forms: holes, slashes, cross-hatching, waves, circles, triangles, squares, and parallel lines. A glyph carries an idea or relationship rather than a sequence of phonemes. Later glyphs may qualify earlier glyphs, so a reader repeatedly revisits the developing meaning until the entire formulation is complete.

Paired lines mark formal emphasis. Protocol normally records only the double-line layer; stronger spoken `!!` and `!!!` force is recovered from context rather than by multiplying the lines indefinitely. The writing can preserve highly precise legal and conceptual relationships across dynasties, but it cannot reproduce the whole auditory and bodily experience of an ancestral court.

##### VARGSH SPEECH THROUGH THE FOUR IMPERIAL PEOPLES

- **uVargsh / pre-Osshiliikkal:** long hisses, clicks, pops, low thrums, and agglutinated formulations carry meaning through audible and physical channels. Humaniki cannot reproduce this reliably.
- **Osshiliikkal:** the first great imperial speech preserves most ancestral registers. Humaniki transcriptions begin using doubled consonants, `gh`, `kh`, and vowel length as approximations, while native concept-glyphs remain the reliable bridge between populations.
- **Mansiikal:** court and technical registers standardize more audible compensations because mixed imperial populations and subject peoples need repeatable commands and records. Older morphologies can still hear distinctions which younger Vargsh increasingly miss.
- **Irishtu:** more distinctions become fully oral. Resonance remains prestigious and useful in formal speech, but the language gap between surviving ancestral morphologies and younger speakers has become an ordinary social problem.
- **Loash:** ordinary court speech can function without sub-sonics and is increasingly compatible with Humaniki articulation. Many Loash can no longer hear or produce the ancestral registers, while ancestral Vargsh cannot reliably produce the Humaniki-influenced speech of their descendants. Glyphs, interpreters, or intermediate languages are often required even within Vargsh society.

Established forms such as **Osshiliikkal, Mansiikal, Irishtu, Loash, Ssilikk, Mansilikgh, Thessilgikh, Salasish,** and **Heshk-ngHesh** remain fixed historical names. Do not reroll them.

##### FORM RULES

1. Keep `ss`, `sh`, `gh`, and `kh` distinct. They carry contrasts which partly replace older hiss or resonance distinctions.
2. Permit initial `ngh-` only in Vargsh names. Humaniki speakers normally add a weak vowel before it when speaking Accented Vargsh.
3. If identical consonants meet at a morpheme boundary, keep a single long consonant: `ss + s` becomes `ss`; `kk + k` becomes `kk`.
4. If `gh` or `kh` meets another fricative, keep the deeper fricative and drop the weaker one unless the result is an established historical spelling.
5. A generated name should normally have two to four audible syllables. Ancient resonance does not count as another syllable.
6. For a pre-Osshiliikkal form, use `!` and `#` only when the GM needs to transcribe the ancestral low-frequency registers. Do not invent an alphabetic letter for the sub-sonic channel.

### Boren Name Generator (Linguistically Grounded)
*For speakers of the Boren city-state and surrounding river valleys. All names derive from A Polski roots drifted through the Borensk lineage (A Polski → B Borensk) with Vietnamese-influenced Tingbyet overlay at 3075 BCE (`Boren <- Tingbyet`) and K1 Ithuuikal substrate at 325 BCE revival (`B1 Boren <= K1 Ithuuikal`). Boren exhibits distinctive vowel enrichment, final consonant restriction to stops/nasals/glottal, and monosyllabic core vocabulary—audibly distinct from all neighboring languages.*

---

#### CORE MECHANICS
- Roll **D66** to determine naming pattern
- For each word part, roll **D66** for the part; use semantic domain mainly for interpretation rather than extra live derivation
- After domain selection, assign a **Tingbyet-style classifier**:
  • Human names normally take a **person classifier**
  • Animals/spirits take a **living classifier**
  • Tools, relics, and trade goods take an **object classifier**
  • Long rigid things, rivers, roads, and banners may take a dedicated **shape classifier**
- After the classifier, apply either **one Referent Suffix** or **one Morphological Suffix**, not both
- Apply constraints after assembly:
  • Drop duplicate letters (`tt` → `t`, `aa` → `a`)
  • Max 2 consecutive vowels (drop earlier vowels)
  • Minimum 2 syllables OR 1 syllable with diphthong (`ya`, `yu`, `oi`, `au`)
- **Flow Insertions** (applied ONLY when CCC/CCCC clusters occur):
  `ʔ` (glottal stop) > `ə` (schwa) > `a`  
  *Example: Krst → Krʔst → "Kr'ast"*

3. **Monosyllabic Bias**: If a generated form feels too long, clip toward a shorter, heavier shape rather than adding more derivation.
4. **Classifier First**: In play, classifier choice matters more than subtle suffix behavior.
5. **Keep It Table-Ready**: Do not apply extra hidden derivations beyond these rules during play.

#### WHY THESE WORK LINGUISTICALLY
- **Kā'k**: Reflects A Polski *święty* (holy) → Boren drift `/ɕfjɛntɨ/` → `/kɛːʔk/` via consonant harmony (`/ʃ/` → `/k/` before back vowel) + vowel lengthening + final fricative → glottal stop. Qua would produce **Shen** (/ʃɛn/); Coro **Khen** (/xɛn/); Thaleshi **Fān** (/faːn/).
- **Kāk**: From *król* (king) → `/krul/` → Boren `/kuːk/` via liquid loss (`/l/` → `/∅/`) + final stop restriction (`/∅/` → `/k/`) + vowel lengthening. Unique Boren pattern—no other language shows this liquid→stop shift.
- **Ē'lek**: From *święty las* (holy forest) → `/ɕfjɛntɨ las/` → Boren `/ɛːʔlɛk/` via cluster simplification (`/ʃv/` → `/ʔ/`) + vowel lengthening (`/a/` → `/ɛː/`) + final liquid restriction (`/l/` → `/k/`). Audibly distinct due to glottal stop insertion.
- **Ūkil'k**: From *rzeka* (river) + *uzdrowić* (heal) → `/ʐɛka uzdrɔvit͡ɕ/` → Boren `/uːkilʔk/` via retroflex loss (`/ʐ/` → `/z/` → `/∅/`) + vowel lengthening (`/u/` → `/uː/`) + final consonant cascade (`/t͡ɕ/` → `/k/` → `/ʔk/`). Only Boren exhibits this glottalized final cluster.

#### GM NOTES: LINGUISTIC AUTHENTICITY
1. **No English respelling**: All forms derive from Polish cognates drifted through Borensk lineage with Vietnamese/Tingbyet influence (e.g., *Kā'k* not "Kak")
2. **Final consonant restriction**: Word-finally only stops/nasals/glottal permitted—immediate auditory marker of Vietnamese substrate absent in Qua/Coro/Stask/Thaleshi
3. **Vowel length as primary stress marker**: Long vowels (`ā ē ī ō ū`) mark lexical stress—distinct from Qua's mobile stress, Coro's schwa dominance, Stask's penultimate stress, Thaleshi's vowel length with mutation
4. **Glottal stop insertion**: Systematic `/ʔ/` insertion replaces illegal finals—unique to Boren among surviving languages
5. **Monosyllabic core vocabulary**: Single-morpheme words collapse to CV(C) structure while compounds remain polysyllabic—reflects Vietnamese contact without full typological shift
6. **Phonotactic enforcement**: Tables exclude illegal Boren finals (no `/l/`, `/r/`, `/s/`, `/ʃ/` word-finally); permitted finals strictly `/p t k b d ɡ m n ŋ ʔ/`
7. **Distinct from neighbors**: Boren `/ʔ/` (glottal stop) vs. Qua liquids preserved vs. Coro velars vs. Stask retroflexes vs. Thaleshi vowel length without glottalization

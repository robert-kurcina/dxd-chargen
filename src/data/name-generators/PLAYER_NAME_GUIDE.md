# Using the Name Generators

Choose the language and the kind of thing being named. Roll D6 for the language's naming pattern and D66 for each required word part. Apply the specified referent marker, if required, and any permitted optional marker. Follow the language's Spacer Preferences and form rules. Read the finished name using its English pronunciation aid.

## Referent and meaning

Referent Category tells you what you are naming: a Person (Male, Female or Neutral where applicable), Living being, Object, Shape, Place, Collective or Divine entity, as supported by the particular generator. These are choices of use, not a requirement that every language has a separate marker for each. A neutral personal name can still require a classifier or ending. Domain/Meaning describes the idea expressed by the name, while station, ritual potential and lineage describe its social or sacred associations.

| Language | How to mark the name |
|---|---|
| Arikkah, Coro, Rasiya | Choose the applicable referent suffix from the language's table. |
| Boren | Choose the classifier first; apply optional human sex endings or station marker only where appropriate. |
| Borensk | Follow the Initial/Kernel/Ending recipe, including votive prefix or compound ending where rolled. |
| Sarek, Qua, Heimneshi, Kahadi | Use the documented civil/household endings where applicable. |
| Stask | Ordinary personal names are neutral by default; complete the regular name with the prescribed domain ending. The separately documented experimental compound lane is not a regular D6 recipe. |
| Thaleshi | Use the detachable va-/ve- particle where applicable; follow the core-name recipe. |
| Blacktongue, Cher-gulo, Fayeed, Ithuuikal | Follow the liturgical recipe; select applicable station, potential or lineage markers, not a generic male/female suffix. |

| P3 Orukugu | Choose the native individual, household, lineage, office, territory or collective ending. Reserve late military loans for appropriate contexts. |
| Vargsh (Ershthiikal) | Choose the native courtly name suffix for the intended use; Humaniki readings cannot reproduce ancestral resonance. |

## English pronunciation key

| Cue | Approximate English sound |
|---|---|
| ah | father |
| eh | bed |
| ee | see |
| oo | food |
| uh | unstressed vowel in about |
| kh | Scottish loch |
| zh | measure |
| capitalized syllable | primary stress, when known |
| hyphen between syllables | pronunciation boundary, not native spelling |
| apostrophe | glottal stop where the language specifies it |

Use the language-specific pronunciation notes for nasal vowels, vowel length, retroflexion and pitch. An English approximation is not an exact IPA transcription and does not change the spelling. A fragment is not a complete name; do not pronounce an invented spacer unless the form rules insert it.

## Reading the tables

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Form / Name | Native spelling; keep its diacritics. |
| IPA | Precise phonetic notation, when supplied. |
| English Pronunciation | Approximate reading for English speakers. |
| English Gloss | Meaning, or source-root meaning for a word part. |
| Referent Category | The kind of entity named, not the semantic domain. |
| Marker | The actual prefix, suffix, classifier, particle or compound construction applied. |

Examples from the existing definitions: Boren Ke-kāk (Person; ke- classifier), Ko-ruk (Living; ko- classifier); Thaleshi Fān (approximately faahn; long vowel). A pronunciation or meaning not established by the source should remain unspecified rather than guessed.

### Reading word-part pronunciation

The English Pronunciation columns approximate the supplied IPA, not the spelling. Adjacent vowel cues can represent separate vowel qualities or a glide; follow the IPA and the language's form rules. Labels such as retroflex, nasal, aspirated, breathy, voiceless, long vowel and high pitch retain distinctions English spelling cannot express. `th` may be voiced or unvoiced (consult IPA); `gh` approximates a voiced velar fricative; `hl` approximates a voiceless lateral fricative. These aids are fragment-level and do not determine stress in a completed name. A completed-name reading must apply the language-specific stress and assembly rules first.

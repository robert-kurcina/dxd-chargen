#### CRITICAL RULES
1. **Beatification Freeze Enforcement**: No phonetic decay occurred after 2550 BCE. All forms reflect phonology frozen at beatification moment:
   - Vowel length fully preserved (`ā ē ī ō ū`)
   - Pitch accent system intact (high tone marks sacred status)
   - Strict CV(C) syllable structure enforced (Tegilag/Swawali influence)
   - *No modal variants exist for Cher-gulo (violates beatification constraint)*

2. **Pitch Accent System**: High tone (`á é í ó ú`) marks sacred status; all Cher-gulo words carry high tone on long vowels:
   - Monosyllables: high tone on long vowel (`kā́`)
   - Disyllables: high tone on first long vowel (`kā́nā`)
   - *Orthographic convention: Acute accent marks high tone on long vowels (ā́ ḗ ī́ ṓ ū́)*

3. **Vowel Length Preservation**: All vowels lengthened at beatification moment:
   - Short vowels → long vowels (`a` → `ā`, `e` → `ē`, etc.)
   - Diphthongs preserved with length (`au` → `aū̯`)
   - *Only Ershthikal shares vowel modification, but Cher-gulo uses lengthening vs. Ershthikal's nasalization*

4. **CV(C) Syllable Enforcement**: Tegilag/Swawali influences enforce strict syllable structure:
   - Permitted: CV, CVn, CVm, CVl, CVr
   - Forbidden: CCV, CCC, CVC with non-liquid/nasal coda
   - *Clusters simplified via epenthesis or deletion before beatification*

5. **Yutskoro Moraic Influence**: Japanese dead substrate enforces moraic timing:
   - Long vowels = 2 moras (`ā` = CV:V)
   - Nasal codas = 2 moras (`an` = CV:N)
   - Creates rhythmic regularity absent in non-beatified languages

6. **No Modal Variants**: Per beatification rules, Cher-gulo has **no** +War/+Lingo/+Barter forms. Any request for modal variants must be redirected to non-beatified languages (Qua, Coro, etc.).

7. **Liturgical-Only Lexicon**: All vocabulary relates to temple life—no secular terms exist in canonical Cher-gulo. Secular concepts borrow from neighboring languages (Qua, Thaleshi) with phonetic adaptation.

8. **Duplicate Letter Dropping**:  
   `tt` → `t`, `aa` → `ā` (lengthened), `rr` → `ɾ` (flap)
